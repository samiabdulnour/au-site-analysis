    How it works

    There is no AI in the drawing. Every line is placed by code from a government dataset, so the same address always produces the same sheet.

    01

    Find the lot

    The address is matched against the NSW address register, then the cadastre gives the lot boundary — including multi-lot sites, where several parcels are combined.

    02

    Gather the context

    Zoning, height and floor-space controls, heritage, terrain, roads, transport, aerial imagery and street detail are fetched for the area around the site.

    03

    Compose the sheet

    Everything is projected, scaled and drawn in the office's graphic standard, with labels placed to avoid collisions, then printed to a print-ready A1 PDF.

    04

    Export for CAD

    The same geometry is written to DXF in real-world metres on the MGA2020 grid, one layer per data class, ready to underlay a survey in ArchiCAD or AutoCAD.

    Where the data comes from

    All of it is public. Nothing is scraped from a screenshot, and no third party is asked what you searched for.

    SourceWhat it provides
    NSW Spatial Services Address register, cadastral lot boundaries, roads and rail, elevation and contours, aerial imagery
    NSW ePlanning Land zoning, maximum building height, floor space ratio, minimum lot size, heritage items
    Transport for NSW Bus stop locations and route information
    OpenStreetMap Bus and light-rail routes, trees, driveways, kerbside parking, street furniture
    Valhalla Walking catchments — how far you actually get on foot in 5 and 10 minutes

    What it does not do

    Read this before you rely on it

    • Not a survey. Boundaries come from the cadastre, which is a mapping approximation. Site areas typically differ from the surveyed figure by a few per cent. Levels come from a 1 m elevation model, not a site survey.
    • Not a planning certificate. The controls shown are the mapped LEP layers. A s10.7 certificate from council is the authoritative document.
    • Council DCP controls are not included. Setbacks, landscaped area and parking rates live in council PDFs that are not published as data, so those rows are left blank for you to complete.
    • Street detail is volunteered. Trees, driveways and parking come from OpenStreetMap and vary in completeness between suburbs.

    Who it is for

    • Architects and building designers — the DA base drawings, in minutes rather than a day, with CAD geometry to design over.
    • Owners and buyers — an honest picture of what the planning controls allow on a property before committing to it.
    • Anyone curious about a site — zoning, transport, walkability and terrain on one readable sheet.

    Coverage

    • New South Wales, Australia. Addresses in the Sydney basin resolve fastest; the rest of the state works but leans on slower public services.
